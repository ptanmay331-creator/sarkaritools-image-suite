How to prepare your photo

  1. Read the current application notice and note the required format, width and height, resolution (if specified), file-size range, background, and any photo-date rules.
  2. Open the resizer and paste the notice text if you want the helper to look for dimensions, DPI, and KB values. Check every value it fills in against the notice; the helper can misread unfamiliar wording.
  3. Choose your photo, set or correct the specifications, and process it. Preview the result and download the JPEG.
  4. Check the downloaded file's dimensions and size, then confirm it is accepted by the official application portal before submitting.

Important photo requirements

There is no single passport-photo size or file-size limit used by every recruitment, exam, or application portal. Requirements can differ between applications and can change. Follow the latest instructions from the organization receiving your application, including any rules about face position, background, image age, and whether a signature or date must appear in the image.

For Indian government recruitment, find the notification on the issuing body's own website—for example, Staff Selection Commission or Union Public Service Commission. These are examples, not a complete list of authorities.

Not an official service: SarkariTools is not affiliated with any government department or recruitment body and does not verify application rules. Always trust the current official notice over preset or automatically detected values.

What the resizer can and cannot do

  • It can crop or fit an image to selected pixel dimensions, adjust brightness and contrast, sharpen edges, and compress a JPEG toward a requested size range.
  • It cannot guarantee that a photo meets an authority's composition, identity, or acceptance rules. Image encoders can also produce slightly different file sizes in different browsers.
  • Files are limited to 20 MB and images to 16 megapixels to help limit browser memory use. Very large requested output dimensions are rejected.
  • Sharpening may make edges look crisper; it cannot recover detail that was never captured by a blurry or low-resolution photo.
